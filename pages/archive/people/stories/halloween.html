Halloween by Eric Timon

Written for Rally 15 by Eric Timon

HALLOWE'EN Once upon a time when Ireland was covered all over with forests and woods there was a King ruling the Country called Euch. He was not a good King. He lived in a large castle in the middle of the Country and his Army spent most of their time going around to see that all the peasants sent one-eight of everything they reaped, collected or reared into the King's Castle. The King was very vain and did a lot of entertaining. If anyone who looked important visited him he entertained them with music and feasting and the peasants had to send their eldest daughters up to the castle to help with the entertaining. The King never married. He had one passion and that was Blackberry Wine. He drank it at all meals and in between meals. He had a special group down in the castle dungeons who did nothing else except make Blackberry Wine.

It so happened that there was a spate of visitors one year and the Chief of the Wine Makers asked to see the King. He told the King that stocks of the Blackberry Wine were running very low and if many more visitors arrived it was possible that the wine would run out. The King was furious. He called his army General and he gave him orders to go out and get his men to warn every peasant in the land that in the coming season, no-one, but no-one was to eat even one blackberry. All the blackberries without any exception were to be collected and brought into the castle. Anyone found taking blackberries for themselves or even eating one would be imprisoned and tortured. When the peasants heard this from the King's men they were dismayed. They liked their Blackberry Pies and Wine. There was uproar among them. They held meetings and more meetings but all of them were afraid of the King's men and no-one could decide what to do. A deputation was sent into the Deep Woods where a wise man called Ian lived. When he arrived it was all explained to him and his advice was asked. He went away and said he would be back in a few days. That night when the moon was full Ian went into the Deep Woods until he came to a dead Elder Tree. He knocked three times on the tree and out from behind it came a Leprechaun. Ian explained to the Leprechaun what was going on and asked for help as no-one could decide what to do. The Leprechaun said he would have to ask the Banshee and Ian could return next night for an answer. The next night Ian did the same routine and the Leprechaun came out and told him that the Banshee wanted more information before deciding to help. She wanted to know the exact date that the King had decided to collect all the blackberries into the Castle. Ian went back to the peasants and told them. They asked all their friends who were working in the Castle to find out. However they found that it was common knowledge in the Castle that the King had decided to have the blackberries all collected on the First Day of November. This was now only a few weeks away. The King issued an order to this effect and had all his armies out day and night watching that no-one was taking any of the blackberries. All the Peasants were going around with long faces not knowing what was going to happen. When it came near the time to collect the blackberries Ian again visited the Leprechaun in the Deep Woods to check that all was well. The Leprechaun re-assured him but told him to warn the peasants that the remedy to outdo the King would be on-going and would be repeated every year from then on. They all agreed.

The first of November dawned bright and clear and King Euch and his friends escorted by his Army came out to supervise the collecting of the blackberries. When they came to the first hedgerow of berries they all stopped and then one of the visitors said "Oh! Euch", and what a sight it was. Each and every blackberry was a mushy, horrible mess. Just to look at them made a person feel sick. The "Banshee" had enlisted the aid of the POOKAS and they had done their work well. There wasn't one single blackberry left unharmed. (From then on a new expression was coined! At the sight of anything extremely unpleasant one exclaimed "Oh! Yuck"). Especially appropriate if one steps in a "cowshout" when crossing a field.

Every tree and every bush in the land was full of "POOKAS" and they were all well equipped for the work they had to carry out for the "Banshee". They all had long noses. Big mouths and several "appendages" for destroying the blackberries. They blew their noses on them. They spat on them. They piddled on them and they "shat" on them.

The King was furious. He stamped his feet and tore his hair and returned to the castle to plan how to find out and punish those responsible. It was no use. His wise Counsellors told him that the Little People were involved and it would be better to play it calmly. He found out that Ian had something to do with it so he sent for him. Ian told him that the Little People had arranged for the Pookas to carry out the deed as they were not pleased with his order about taking all the Blackberries. The King asked Ian to tell the Little People that he was sorry and he wanted things back the way that they were before. Ian carried the message to the Leprechaun in the Deep Woods but the Leprechaun said that's alright but as agreed from now on the Pookas will destroy all the blackberry crop in the entire country on the First Day of November every year. The crops will have to be collected before then. Ian told the peasants the news. They were all delighted. He then told the king and he was relieved and glad that he would have Blackberry Wine back again in the future. From then on no matter where Ian went in the country he was recognised and crowds gathered around him shaking his hands, patting him on the back and shouting "Hail O Ian"!! In time this became abbreviated into "Hallowe'en" and that's how the 31st of October night became known as "Hallowe'en Night" ("Hello Ian" night)!

Of course nobody in Ireland ever eats a blackberry from the 1st November. N.B. All pooka owners note that their pookas will be missing (spelt messing) on 31st October every year.
