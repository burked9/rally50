The Boat

This story by Eric Timon was published in the Derg Rally 21 Magazine.

Once upon a time there was a young boy called Sean O'Siocran. He was an Orphan. His parents were killed in a car-crash when he was four years old and he had lived with his aunt and uncle ever since. They owned a large farm which was on the shore of a large river. As Sean grew up his uncle taught him all about animals, birds, fish, insects, etc. Sean grew to love them and he spent a lot of time watching them. He wouldn't dream of harming any of them. When he fished he let the small ones go back into the water and the large ones he killed immediately before taking them home to his Aunt to be cooked.

He went to the local school and as he was so gentle in his own way he had to put up with a lot of bullying and teasing from some of the other boys in his class. They all knew of his regard for animals, birds, insects, etc., and they constantly teased him about it. Nevertheless, it didn't change him in any way.

When he left school he took over all the heavy farm work from his uncle who was now getting on in years. He made a success of the farm and the three of them lived a frugal but happy existence on it. Despite repeated requests Sean wouldn't allow anyone on the farmland to shoot or hunt. As time went by the farm became a Sanctuary to all the animals and birds who came to it.

Both his aunt and uncle fell victim to an outbreak of "flu" and despite Hospital Treatment they both died. Sean was devastated. He was now all alone on the farm. He managed to eke out a living from it but there were no luxuries.

One day some of his former school-mates who had been refused permission to shoot or hunt on the farm decided to play a trick on him. They put a scrap boat which had lain unused for years in the open where it had rotted on a trailer and at dead of night they took it to the shore of the river on Sean's farm and left it there. When Sean saw it he was delighted but when he pushed it into the water it just sank immediately. Then he knew that a trick had been played on him. He was very sad and disappointed. He went for a walk into the nearby wood. After a while he heard a tapping sound and thinking it was a woodpecker he followed the sound. It came from the back of a large dead Elder Tree. When he went around to the far side of the tree he was dumbfounded for what did he see but a LEPRECHAUN mending a tiny boot and the tap of his tiny hammer was the sound that Sean had heard.

The LEPRECHAUN looked up and said "Hello Sean you're not very happy to-day, you have a boat on your mind". "That's right", said Sean, and he told the LEPRECHAUN all about the boat. The LEPRECHAUN said, "Sean did you ever hear tell of POOKA JUICE?" Sean said, "No, never". "Well", said the LEPRECHAUN, "It's made from the fruit of the Elderberry Tree to a secret POOKA JUICE RECIPE. You get some empty bottles and leave them here tonight. Come back in the morning and I'll tell you what to do". With that the LEPRECHAUN stood up, walked around the tree and disappeared.

Sean hurried home and searched for empty bottles. He found six. He washed them thoroughly and back he went to the Elder tree and left the bottles at the base of it. Next morning he headed out for the tree and when he arrived there the LEPRECHAUN was waiting for him. All the bottles were full of a dark red liquid. The LEPRECHAUN said, "Sean, you keep one bottle for yourself. Drink one glass of POOKA JUICE every day. The other five bottles you must use to paint the bottom of the boat that will be in the water. Use all the Pooka Juice and when it's all used up leave it for one day and then launch the boat. Go and enjoy yourself, Sean", said the Leprechaun and getting up he walked round the Elder Tree and disappeared.

Sean hurried home and got ready to paint the bottom of the boat with the Pooka Juice. First he drank the glass himself then he started painting. The Pooka Juice just soaked into the dry wood and by nightfall he had only used one bottle of the juice, so it took him five days to drink his bottle of Pooka Juice and the same five days to paint the bottom of the boat. At last he had it done and ready to launch. He anxiously waited the day that the Leprechaun had told him to wait before launching the boat. The day dawned bright and clear. The boat was only about fifty yards from the water. He used the same method as before to launch it by putting round pieces of wood under the front of it and pushing it forward. He soon had it in the water and getting into it he was soon rowing all over the river with the makeshift oars he had. He was deliriously happy.

Day after day Sean took the boat out on the river. He visited islands that he could only look at before this. He fished a lot and fish became his main diet. His so-called friends were amazed when they saw what had happened and they decided to take the boat. They watched from a distance and when Sean had left the boat they move in. All four of them got into the boat and pushed it out from the shore. When they had rowed about ten yards from the shore water started leaking into the boat and within minutes the boat had sunk leaving them all floundering in the water. They had to wade a shore and wet and weary they had to make their way home to explain how they got soaked.

When Sean came down next morning the boat was where he left it and he just carried on. Next day one of the so-called friends called on Sean and told him what had happened. Sean was puzzled but had a good laugh over it. Day after day Sean used the boat but day after day he noticed that the boat got harder and harder to row as if it was getting heavier and heavier. Eventually it was so heavy that he just couldn't row it anymore.

He decided to take it out of the water to examine it so like when he launched it he got some round pieces of wood and a rope. He put the round pieces of wood under the front of the boat and pulled hard on the rope. The boat moved out of the water and was soon about fifty yards on shore. He heaved and heaved and eventually got the Boat turned over so that he could examine the bottom. He was amazed to find that the POOKA JUICE had hardened like metal and was very thick. He went into the blacksmith who knew all about metals and asked him to have a look at it. The blacksmith was intrigued with Sean's story about the boat and the Pooka-juice so he went out with Sean to have a look at it. He cleaned off a part of it and examined it then he let a great yell out of him. "Sean, It's Gold! Sean, It's Gold. It's Gold!" Sean didn't know what to do. The blacksmith was a very honest man and as well as that when he thought about what happened to the four who tried to rob the boat from Sean he knew that the Leprechauns wouldn't put up with any skullduggery so he removed all the gold from the boat and took it into the local Bank Manager. He converted the gold into cash which he put into Sean's account. Sean was now a very rich man. First of all he gave a very handsome reward to the blacksmith. Then he arranged to purchase more of the land along the river. He had a new cottage built for himself and then he turned all his land into a reserved park where everyone could come in and have a picnic and watch the birds and the animals in their natural surroundings. He employed Rangers who moved among the visitors and made sure that none of the birds or animals were interfered with. Anyone who threw a stone at a bird or animal was immediately put out of the park and was never admitted again.

Sean lived to a ripe old age in his cottage. His will ensured that his reserved park would be looked after for ever by Rangers. Even though he often went into the woods where he first met the Leprechaun he never met him again so he came to the conclusion that the Leprechaun was happy with the way he was looking after his wishes. When Sean died they put up a Monument to him and it was surrounded by all the animals, birds, fish, insects, etc., as a reminder to all those who visited the Park that nature was beautiful and shouldn't be interfered with.

Eric Timon
