Rally 25 (2000): Lough Derg Rallies (A Potted History)

This article was published for the Derg Rally 25 Magazine in 2000.

Lough Derg Rallies (A Potted History)

Although Rally One was in 1975, from the branch minute books early "stirrings" can be found as far back as February 1971. At that meeting the Chairman, John Ryan, considered that the Shannon Rally had outlived its attractions and it was time that Lough Derg should become better known by boat hirers. Possible sponsors of a rally on Lough Derg would be Esso and Carroll's Cigarettes. A sub-committee was formed and quickly decided on the weekend of 26/27 June for a rally of 15 to 20 boats at Kilgarvan. The owner of Fool's Island and Mr Huskisson of Waterloo Lodge were approached to cater for over-spill from the tiny Kilgarvan Quay and it was felt that all three could accommodate 15 boats overnight. Kilgarvan Angling Club were creating extra moorings at the time and a collection through the rally raised £13 for the club in appreciation of the hospitality shown to the rally. At the AGM of October 1971, it was reported that members had joined in a week-long rally of Emerald Star boat.

In 1972 it was planned to visit Dromineer, Kilgarvan and Scariff and it was felt 10/11 boats might well be the maximum that could be comfortably moored during night stops — additional boats would be welcome but would have to face the risk of finding alternative moorings for the nights. A second rally was held that year on 8/9 September visiting Scariff and Kilgarvan. The difficulty experienced in finding the entrance to the Scariff river in the face of a setting sun prompted the Branch to complain to the O.P.W. and look for improvements in markers and buoys.

In 1973 the first weekend in June was again picked with an ambitious route planned. Friday would be spent in Killaloe to allow Dublin based members time to get their boats organised. The fleet would move to Scariff for a barbecue and an inspection of boats and gear, and then on to Mountshannon. Kilgarvan, Terryglass and Dromineer were also to be visited and although not all ports received a call the seeds of a big one were definitely sown. The October minutes suggested a seven day rally for 1974 to include boats from Athlone.

The following year 1974, saw the rally again take in the early June weekend with calls to Kilgarvan where they shared in Marty and Bridie O'Meara's celebrations in opening their new lounge. A barbecue was held in Garrykennedy with much local approval and a request for a repeat performance later in the year. The meeting of 30th August was so taken by Capt. Knight's win on the Shannon Rally that they decided to have a celebration mini-rally on 28/29 September with Capt. Knight and his crew as guests at a lunch in the "Sail-in" in Dromineer with the menu secured at £1.50 per person.

The rally in 1975 was pencilled-in for the week after the August bank holiday and a sub-committee was formed for what was to evolve into Lough Derg Rally One. The June meeting was told of 29 entries to date and plans moving-on. The Rally secretaries report to the September meeting told the amazing story of 84 entries. Only one boat was hired and with 35 boats from Lough Derg members, the numbers were made up from other branches showing the growing popularity of the lake. Fees were £2 for "small boats", £4 for medium and £6 for large. The budget was tight for this huge event and the balance was helped by a Board Failte donation of £100. Some trophies were donated but it was felt more should be purchased. The week had favourable weather except for two mornings when departure was delayed to avoid upsetting visitors from the upper Shannon, some of whom were heard to say they had never seen white caps inland before. The closing supper had 272 people and kind comments came from far and wide about the great week. There was a complaint about too few people doing too many jobs (committees!!!), but the commitment was there to keep this ball rolling.

The following year's rally was just as successful but scant details are found in the minutes. Entries realised £341 and adverts brought in a further £140. The children were well catered for and surplus brochures were to be sold for 50p each. Only one bad cheque (£2) was received and the bank strike was blamed and the money was repaid. In June 1977 concern was raised regarding petrol engined boats and the Vice-Commodore was to study arrangements for their safe berthing. A gift of a fire extinguisher was discussed and on a lighter note it was reported that chip vans would attend at Mountshannon and Kilgarvan. The head count on the 1977 rally was 274 adults and 100 children and another great week was reported with the rally now starting a day earlier — on Monday.

In 1978 tickets for the rally dinner in Killaloe were £4 for adults and £2 for children. Plaques that were worn procured at £2 each and brochure adverts cost £24 per page, £12 per half page and £6 per quarter. A ladies committee and junior committee were formed to help organise this now mammoth event. The music at the Lakeside Hotel was to be provided by Eddie La Touche from 10pm to 1.30am for a fee of £50. A cheese and wine event was arranged for the first time due to an unfortunate event at the previous rally dinner and wine was to be purchased with a ratio of 3 white to 1 red. The summer of 1978 was affected by a postal strike and fuel shortage.

There was a disaster in 1979 when the water level on the lake had to be lowered due to a breach near O'Brien's Bridge. The Rally was cancelled at a meeting in July with all of the preparations already in place.

The 1980's minutes are very comprehensive and often run to a couple of thousand words for rally meetings alone. It was a time of much discussion on insurances etc., and at this time the I.W.A.I. itself was organising itself into a limited company. Large rallies moving around the lake were putting pressure on existing facilities and today's amenities to an extent are the result of constant lobbying by the Branch. The showing of the Ruth Heard films during winter meetings was increasing interest in our waterways and also giving the uninitiated a picture of the mad ralliers. Reports in the minutes of other I.W.A.I. rallies and notification of rallies to come show the members growing interest in greener pastures.

The December 1984 A.G.M. heard a proposal for a cruise in company to Shannonbridge to celebrate the 1,500th anniversary of the birth of Brendan The Navigator. The plan was to visit Clonfert where the Saint is buried and a sub-committee was elected to put this idea in motion. The Offaly Branch joined in the planning of what was to be a major event on the 1985 calendar. Forty boats were to gather at Shannonbridge and the "Brendan Voyage" was a resounding success despite the insurance problems in the I.W.A.I. at the time. Incidentally, the 1980's minutes are in the colourful hands of Maureen Duffy — all hand written and a living treasure. Quoting verbatim from the A.G.M. 8/12/1984: "The officers of Rally 10 were elected as follows — Commodore Chris (Loud Hailer) Boyle, Vice-Commodore Joe (4E) Treacy, Rear-Commodore John (Trapper John) Martin".

The rally itself in 1985 powered on in the usual Lough Derg tradition complete with "Barge Race!!". Wild and wet weather and for the first time the loss of the Commodore's boat. Chris Boyle in his report thanked the rescuers and took the inevitable ditty (you picked a fine time to leave be Christine) on the chin. The Commodores boat on Rally 11 again suffered the wrath of the weather and a certain quay wall on the Scariff river but enough on that subject.

I could go on and on but because of the danger of boring the pants off you I will call a halt to all this history. My first Lough Derg rally was Rally Three in 1977 (David Knight as Commodore) and it certainly laid the foundations for many wonderful rallies. Lots of the early people have passed on — hopefully still rallying in another life. The helpfulness and friendship over the years from people who were once complete strangers never ceases to amaze me. The committees through the years have voluntarily given blood, sweat and tears and more so that the annual shop window of Lough Derg Branch would look its best. The Commodores of course must always be remembered for being ultimately responsible for a good or bad week. A bad week on Lough Derg — never.

JOE TREACY

P.S.: I invite anyone else to borrow the minute books and perhaps write articles on toilet blocks, markers, moorings, County Councils etc., etc. The information is all there — sheer bliss!!!
