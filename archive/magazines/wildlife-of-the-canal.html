Derg Rally 24 (1999): Wildlife of the Canal

Written for the Derg Rally 24 Magazine in 1999 by Aisling Moore.

Wildlife of the Canal Written by Aisling Moore

Otter The otter is an aquatic carnivore, found almost worldwide. In the common species distributed throughout Europe and Asia, the body maintains a length of 75 cm (30 inches), with the tail half as long as the body. The head is broad and flat with short, rounded ears; the blunt snout bears lateral slit-like nostrils. The ears and nostrils can be closed when the animal is diving. The fur is chestnut, the legs are short but strong and the webbed feet have claws.

Otters live in dens, usually by the side of the water and with an underwater entrance to the den. They feed on fish and also on small mammals, birds, frogs and crayfish. The animals breed in the spring and authorities suggest a gestation period of about 61 days. The litter ranges from one to five pups, which stay with the mother for about a year.

Salmon These fish have elongated bodies covered with small rounded scales and a fleshy fin between the dorsal fin and tail. They are valuable 'food' fish and excellent game fish. They can be found in salt and freshwater. Many go from salt to fresh water to breed and then the young go from fresh to salt water then they reach maturity. The migratory instinct of members of the salmon family is remarkably specific, each generation returning to spawn in exactly the same breeding places as the generation before it. Even those who don't migrate breed in the same place as their ancestors. The spawning ground is usually in a rapid flowing, clear stream with gravel and rocks beneath it. Their colouring is usually drab before mating which varies with the species, members of the same family develop bright hues at spawning time. They have great economic value. Anglers use a rod and reel but commercial fishing for salmon is done on a much larger scale; employing traps and pound nets to catch the fish on the way to their spawning ground.

Mosquito Common name for some 2,000 species. They have long slender wings and a narrow body. Females like to feed on warm-blooded animals, humans included. When they bite, they inject some salivary fluids into the wound causing swelling and irritation i.e. lots of frantic itching. They have spread many diseases including malaria, yellow fever, dengue and filariasis.

Females lay eggs only in water, some in running water, others in woodland pools, marshes, swamps etc. The larvae are known as 'wrigglers' because of their wriggling motion in the water. Mosquitoes may be controlled by eliminating the breeding places or spraying those places with oil or insecticides.

Kingfisher This is a common name for any member of family of birds having large heads, often crested. Their bodies are short and plump, and their legs are small. Most of them have short tails and their bills are variable, depending on their food. Not all kingfishers feed on fish; a large number of forest-inhabiting species eat non-aquatic invertebrates and small vertebrates. Kingfishers are found in the temperate and tropical areas of the world. They rest in tunnels and in earth banks and lay pure white eggs. They range in size from 10 cm (4 inches) to 46 cm (18 inches) long and can be found perched on branches of trees.

Heron They developed from the stork order, found in wet areas in all temperate and tropical parts of the world. Herons have elongated necks, legs and bills. Cranes, which resemble herons, fly with their necks extended. Herons fly with their necks in an 'S' shape with their heads supported in between their shoulders. Their bills are sharp-edged and pointed. They have 4 long clawed toes on each foot; 3 forward, 1 behind. The claw in the middle of the forward toes has a rough comb-like inner margin that is used in preening soft plumage. Most species construct their loose, flat, platform-like nests high in the branches of swamp trees. Eggs of herons are pale blue and laid in numbers of two to six. They mainly eat fish but have been known to catch mice.
