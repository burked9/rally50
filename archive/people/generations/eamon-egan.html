Legends of the Lake: Eamon Egan

Legends of the Lake

Eamon Egan in interview with Ivan Thornbury

When did you first get interested in boats? I grew up around Shannonbridge where my Dad had a fishing boat but my mother wouldn’t let me go in it. She wouldn’t let me out on the water until I had my First Communion so my first Communion day was my first time out in the fishing boat! My father had land that was on both sides of the river up there so we’d be herding cattle going backwards and forwards and I used to go fishing. My brothers used to go fishing as well but I would have gone out more than the rest of them.

So when did you start working with boats? I did my Leaving Cert in 1969; I was out of school looking for a job when the cruisers started working on the Shannon. I was looking at them going past. Guinness’s had started up in 1970 and somebody I knew had some contact there and said I should try and get a job there. I remember being interviewed by John Le Froy outside Killeen’s pub sometime in 1970. John was the manager though he would only have been 26 or 27. Anyhow I got the job and from there it went.

So was that in Portumna? Well! Guinness started in Carrick and I worked up there for a month of two, starting in March 1971 but then they opened in Portumna which is where I had applied for a job. I was doing a bit of everything, painting, fuelling and breakdowns. There were only two or three of us there at the start but they grew the fleet very quickly in the 1970’s and would take on a lot of lads out of school over the summer holidays.

When did you get your own first boat? In the beginning, I wouldn’t even have had a bicycle. I was earning £11.50 a week and after 5 or 6 years working I got my first boat. It was a speed boat and then around 1979 I got Hawthorne. She was originally a seaplane-refuelling tender built during the second World War, but had been fitted out as sand suction dredger. She had been working up in Ballina, in Mayo, keeping the harbour clear of sand bars. She was flat decked when I got her but over a few years I put her together as I had the price of the bits of steel... I built on the front deck first then the front cabin, rear cabin and wheel house.

Is it the original engine? No, there was an old engine in her but the guy who sold it wanted to keep it and took it out. It was a single lorry engine in the middle even though she was twin screw. There was the lorry gearbox with shaft out the back and then a chain around the two shafts. There was a clutch and you’d put it into 3rd gear and off she’d go. We took it out for a spin like this before I bought it.

So, how long did you stay in that job? I was sixteen years with Guinness’s which of course became Emerald Star. Then I went to work for Brian Cullen in Killaloe. Martina and I had got married the year before and I wanted a change of job. Brian had the marina and the Derg cruiser hire; I stayed 5 years then in 1990 went to work for Reggie Goodbody in Shannon Yacht Fitters back in Portumna. This was different in that it wasn't in the tourist industry; you'd go in in the morning and be working all day every day on boats. Reggie fitted out boats for Emerald star and other customers. We’d build the interiors and fit the engines and all the running gear. The Broom hulls would arrive with the superstructure turned upside down and fitted into the hull to make them more compact for shipping over. We’d lift off the superstructure, fit out the boat and then put the top back on, right ways up! We did the Broom Sedans with the patio door across the back and the AF32’s Lakestar 6 berths. We also fitted out sea angling boats, working from scratch with bigger engines for off-shore. It was very interesting work. Reggie was dead sound and I loved working for him. We started at 8:30 and if you were two minutes late he’d be looking at his watch but then at 5pm you’d be in the middle of job and he’d be roaring at you to go home!

Then in August 2000, Reggie retired and Billy Shaw bought the place. Billy was a buyer and seller of boats and was extraordinarily good at it. He's a super salesman. There were people that bought boats from Billy Shaw that didn't mean to buy boats! I got on very well with Billy and did all his maintenance work at the start but then over time as his son grew up and got involved it kind of suited me and I started my own business. I had a few different people working for me at the start but ended up with two good guys, Fergal and Brendan. I’d known Fergal for a long time; his family used to hire boats from Emerald for their holidays. He was always ferociously interested in how everything worked and as a young kid he'd be asking you questions you wouldn't know the answer to. In the last year I’ve scaled things back to a one man operation and I’ve moved out of Portumna and am working from home.

When did you start doing the Rallies? Before the organised rallies started there used to be what we called Guinness Rallies. Guinness employees would get a big discount on hiring boats and a gang of them would arrive down and go on the tear around the lake for a week. Then the IWAI rallies started and we would have joined when the kids were babies around Rally 9 or 10. Once the girls (Laura, Ciara, Louise and Olivia) got interested you had to go every year. We got great enjoyment out them and met a lot of friends. Then when the kids were sailing, the rally in August used to clash with sailing events and we didn't always make it. The girls used to say they had their friends, their boating friends and their sailing friends. Olivia plans to bring Hawthorne to the rally this year.

Any plans for trips in Hawthorne? We have gone up as far as Kesh and down as far as Kilrush. On the canals we haven't gone that far as it's hard to get the time. I'd like to do the Green and Silver, when the grandkids get a bit older hopefully we'll have them for crew. I'm looking forward to that. At this stage their ages are 3 years old, 21 months, 10 months and the youngest is 4 days old!
