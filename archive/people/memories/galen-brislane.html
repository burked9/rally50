More Rally Memories - Galen Brislane

This was written for rally 37 in 2012

More Rally Memories

Galen Brislane - Crescendo

My lovely wife, Kerry, constantly ribs me regarding the rose-tinted glasses I wear when reminiscing about my childhood spent on the River Shannon. She's South African. She grew up in the shadow of Table Mountain in Cape Town and her parents now live in Durban where the worst days of their winters are better than the best days of our summers. In 2001 I entered Kerry's name in a competition at the Dublin Boat Show. She won a week on a Tara Cruiser, courtesy of Mr Des Gillet. While I was ecstatic to have won a week on one of the beautiful, steel cruisers, Kerry's most vivid memory of the week was steering, from the fly-bridge, in June, wearing full offshore Hely Hansen gear. That’s all very well, but there's a very good reason for my harking back to early days on the Shannon. Quite simply, that reason is the Lough Derg Boat Rallies.

One of my earliest rally memories is of the 1976 rally. My Dad (the aul' fella) owned a Darragh 14 powered by an Evinrude 6 HP. She was too small, in his opinion, to do a rally, so he and my brother (Darrell) travelled in convoy with the rally every day but navigated to our home port (and our Sprite Musketeer caravan) of Garrykennedy each evening. Much to my chagrin my mother deemed me too young to do the rally. I have yet to forgive her.

Fast forwarding a few years to 1979, this was the year of the Rally That Never Was. I was heartbroken to learn there would be no rally this year. What the heck? Whose idea was this? I was so indignant. It didn't dawn on me that if you can walk around the cairn at the entrance to the river at Killaloe or you can actually see the Benjamin Rocks protruding 2 feet above the water level of the lake, there probably wasn't enough water in Lough Derg to stage the rally. Whatever challenges the ESB were having that year, they messed up my summer.

1982 saw the staging of Lough Derg Boat Rally 7. 30 years ago. Hard to imagine. The Commodore was the previously aforementioned aul' fella. His ship was Lady Sarah; a Freeman 23 bought the previous year from Cormacruisers. The rally kicked off at our home port of Garrykennedy and weather-wise the Mediterranean didn't get a look in. Looking out on the lake towards Illaunmore the water was as calm as a mill pond. Come on Eileen by Dexy's Midnight Runners was blasting on the radio from the cockpit of Shannon Pride. Life was good. The weather held, the lake remained calm and in the days pre-Celtic Tiger I perfected my skills 'waterskiing' on a polystyrene body-board behind our dinghy, powered by its 3.5HP Mariner. I was a lot skinnier and lighter in those days.

1983 was when the rallies really started to get interesting. I was on board Nick Coady Snr’s boat, Catalina, in Garrykennedy harbour. A most memorable night as I had my first real snog with a girl – her name was Elaine. The weird thing was my best rally buddies, Nicky Coady Jnr and Mark Maguire Jnr were sitting in the cabin watching us, having a grand ol’ laugh.

1985 saw the staging of Rally 10 - my first brush with alcohol. And sweet cheeses, what a brush it was. The Commodore hosted a cheese & wine reception on Kilgarvan Quay on the opening night of the rally. The aul' fella, as a committee member, asked me to assist. I was to act as a waiter offering cheese and pouring wine. It wasn't quite ‘one bottle for the rally / one bottle for me’ but it might as well have been. Late in the evening the aforementioned Messrs Coady and Maguire and I started walking up to Ballinderry. Half way up that country road things became a blur. At one stage Mark held me under an old style water pump. Most of them, by the mid 80s, were no longer actually connected to the water supply. This one was. Mark held. Nicky pumped. Their amusing attempt to sober me up. I was livid. I swung at Nicky with a clenched fist. A lens popped out of his glasses and smashed on the ground. By now the lads realised I wasn't putting on my drunken stupor. Back to Kilgarvan and to bed. My biggest challenge was the fact Vitesse, our Ocean 30, was moored to the bank, accessible by a narrow gang plank. Slithering on my belly up that plank must have been quite a sight for anyone who witnessed it. Fortunately neither of my parents did!

1986 and the last of my reminiscing. The rally steamed in to Scariff Harbour in fair weather...but things were about to change dramatically. That night in Scariff there was, to all intents and purposes, a cloudburst. It will be difficult to imagine for anyone who didn't witness the event but the morning after the downpour the Scariff River had turned in to a body of white, foaming, churning water. The committee had a difficult decision: to keep to the rally schedule and run the gauntlet or opt for another night in the harbour. I am happy to say Health and Safety wasn't the scourge it is today and we ran the gauntlet.

With people assigned to positions on both banks armed with stakes and long lines, the rally set off. The canal section from the harbour to the river was as calm as could be. Much to my dismay the aul' fella took the helm. We approached the river and ... Cheeses, Mary and Joseph. Vitesse surged up and forward as though we were a rubber raft on a white water expedition. The aul' fella threw the wheel to starboard...then hard to port, countering the boiling waters under us. Then ‘Damn It!’ (He wasn't one to curse). A red-hulled, 19’ sail boat was ahead and her outboard had cut out. She was now being carried on the quick current while spinning in circles. We managed to stay in control and pass her with no contact. What a feat! As I looked back she continued to spin in to a bank where a committee placed, shore crew member got a line to her.

But that wasn't the end of it. Of concern to us most – would we be able to negotiate the bend at Tuamgraney or would the sheer force of the current slam us in to the harbour wall there? Fortunately for us on Vitesse we made the bend in the river safely. The same couldn't be said for one of the barges. An Emerald Star Forest Class with a bunch of lads on board were whooping and hollering as each boat came by, watching the skippers attempting to keep control of their vessels. When the barge (I cannot remember which barge it was) didn't negotiate the bend and just missed making fibreglass smithereens of the Emerald by inches, the boyos cast their lines and headed for the lake to wash their underwear! As for the barge, she arrived in Dromineer with a tarpaulin draped over her bow covering the hole punched by Tuamgraney pier.

But enough about boozing and floozies. They were but a small part of the overall rally experience. But how do you impart that experience to someone who has never done a rally? It’s difficult. On returning home after a rally, people would ask "who won the race?" Boat competitions and barbeques. Dogs' fancy dress and diddly-eye. Family frolicking fun. Maybe Ratty best summed it up in Wind in the Willows when he said to Mole:

"Believe me, my young friend, there is nothing -- absolutely nothing -- half so much worth doing as simply messing about in boats."
