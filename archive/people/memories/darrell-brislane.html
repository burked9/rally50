Rally Memories - Darrell Brislane

This was written for the Magazine for Rally 37 in 2012

Rally Memories

When Michael asked me to do a short piece on my memories of rallies past, I was the picture of calm saying to Michael “sure, not a problem” but underneath it all I was a little worried. The old memory is just not what it used to be. Unlike the brother (see elsewhere in this brochure), who can remember all the registrations of cars we ever owned, what particular event happened in what year and all the girls he kissed, for me memory is a jumble of images with no order to it.

So what is it that I do remember? Well, things like sitting on the quay in Garrykennedy and seeing a floatilla of boats crossing east to west off in the distance. A quick check with dad confirmed it was Rally 1 (1976) with its oddbod grouping of boats. We had a 12” Delcraft dingy with a Seagull outboard at the time so no rally for us, but that was to change for Rally 2.

By then we had a Daragh 14 with a small shell of a cabin – sheer luxury. Off we set, myself and my dad, from Garrykennedy all the way to Terryglass (I think) leaving the rest of the family behind in the caravan. I was well prepared as the night before I had bought my first packet of Fisherman’s Friends. Never bought a packet since.

Two things stuck out for me on that rally – we had to head back to Garry every night as the cabin was too small to sleep in, and we entered the Best Boat competition. I remember the judges coming on board with that “what the..?” type of look on their faces. We had pretty much everything we were supposed to have, but it was just all piled in a heap in the middle of the cockpit. To this day I still believe we were robbed!

Rally 3 saw us upgrade to a Shetland 535 which allowed us to stay with the rally – no more caravans for us. Today when I see a Shetland I still wonder how the dickens a family of five lived and slept on a boat that size for a week, but it was done. That was a feature of the early rallies for us—each year we turned up in a different boat. Dad always knew when it was time to get a new boat…it was always shortly after mum had everything organised on the current one – new curtains up, all space efficiently utilised and then boom…”Guess what I did today dear?”!!

By rally 7 (1982) we had moved up to a Freeman 23 (Lady Sarah) – a style of boat with classic lines that have stood the test of time. That rally was significant for us as it was Dad’s turn as commodore and that’s about all that I can remember from that year. At this point many of the images are beginning to repeat and merge - things like; bagpipes on the quayside in the mornings to rise the slumbering sailors, the smell of bacon cooking from dozens of galleys, messing around in small dinghies, rowing, diving, swimming and generally goofing off. Other memories stuck like buying the groceries out of the boot of a car on Kilgarvan quay, some bloke sticking his head out a porthole to shout at somebody and losing his false teeth as soon as he opened his mouth –splash, gone. I knew outboard motors inside out back then – today I’m still trying to figure out how to get the 40:1 mix right. This was all carried out with the same group of friends – never saw them from one end of the year to the next but day 1 on any rally and we just picked up where we left off from the previous year.

While our boats evolved over time, many others returned year after year. Classics like the Golden Hours and Argent Brac were synonymous with those early rallies as were some of the barges, particularly the St Patrick. Some of the boats were classic in other ways like AMOW, AMOW II, AMOW III and inevitably NAMOW (ask the old timers). I think around 1984 the number of boats on the rally broke the 100 mark for the first time proving quite a challenge for the harbour masters but remember, boats back then though were on average a fair bit smaller than what we see today.

Into the mid-80s, summer jobs became the norm but the boss knew that come the first week of August I was out of there. By now the kids had mutinied and taken over the running of the boat – basically something we had to do as members of the committee could not take part in competitions – and Dad was always a bit of a stickler for the rules. One year we nailed the man overboard and a second competition (that memory issue again) and proudly headed off with trophies under our arms.

On my later rallies, the memory bank shows me sleeping in tents instead of boats, night time sing songs well away from the adults, pool games in a dark back room in the pub in Portumna, buying my first beer and drinking it behind a stone wall with the lads. When looking back, and I suspect many will identify with this, because the rallies took place that same one week every year, they line up as if stepping stones of early life, each year slightly older, slightly bolder.

Inevitably, college, jobs, other interests and finally emigration all managed to get in the way of that week on Lough Derg. Time moved on and I finally got to re-join the rally in 2011, this time with three kids in tow. Here’s hoping they can build up the same type of memories, and in their case remember them!

Darrell Brislane Andante
