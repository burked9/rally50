The Return of The Open Boat - The Stimpson Cup

The Return of The Open Boat

The Stimpson Cup

Every rally has its own stories, traditions and characters, and for years we, as relatively new ralliers, have been hearing stories about the 'Open Boat' as a very special tradition on the Lough Derg Rally. We heard how it has often attracted younger participants and more adventurous crews keen on experiencing the rally in its most fundamental form: travelling light, living in tents and making your own way around in a small open boat. Every time someone told us about it, it was with a little devilish smile and clearly fond recollection.

Long before rallies became somewhat comfortable affairs of hot meals, snug beds and well-stocked larders, there was the idea (or necessity) of trying to manage with less, carrying only what was needed, sleeping under canvas and cooking your own meals. Taking part as an Open Boat also meant moving from harbour to harbour under one's own steam. Part boat handling skills, part expedition and part cheerful stubbornness, the Open Boat tradition sits firmly in that spirit of uncomplicated adventure.

At the centre of that tradition is the re-kindled Open Boat Competition, for which the Stimpson Cup is awarded. For many years the cup was awarded to a participant travelling in a day boat or open boat and though the aspect of the competition gradually faded from the rally programme over time, the cup itself on and off remained part of rally history.

With many interested teens in the youth committee, this year seems like a great opportunity to bring the Open Boat Competition formally back to life. The aim is not hardship for hardship's sake, nor is it intended as a survival exercise. Instead, it is about embracing simplicity, boat handling skills and self-reliance while remaining fully part of the wider rally community.

Participants of the competition will camp, cook for themselves, travel independently and therefore take on the daily rhythm of the rally in a more immediate and elemental way. At the same time, the spirit of the rally remains unchanged and will need crews to look out for one another. Fellow ralliers are encouraged to keep an eye on the Open Boat participants, offer support where needed and share in the camaraderie that has always defined the event. The competition itself will recognise boat handling skills and self-sufficiency, but also environmental stewardship, campcraft, good humour, resilience and contribution to the rally atmosphere.

In many ways, the Open Boat competition is a reminder of what drew many of us to the boating life in the first place: adventure, camaraderie and the satisfaction of making do with little more than good preparation, practical skill and a willingness to embrace whatever the weather and tide may bring.

The Open Boat Competition returns as a celebration of that enduring spirit.
