*Written for Lough Derg Rally Magazine 37 (2013)*

In Ireland Irish fleabane has only ever been recorded on the islands and shores of Lough Derg in north Tipperary and south-east Galway. The species was once found at quite a few sites along the shores of the lake but due to the impact of humans on its habitat the species is now only known to survive at one location. Due to its vulnerability Irish fleabane is protected in Ireland under the Wildlife (Amendment) Act 2000 and is listed as vulnerable in the Irish Red Data Book.

Lough Derg contains the last site in Ireland for the protected plant species Irish Fleabane. To conserve this species and ensure that part of Lough Derg’s and Ireland’s biodiversity is not lost forever, a conservation project was initiated by the community of Terryglass (Co. Tipperary), the National Botanic Gardens and BEC Consultants. This project was sponsored by the Heritage Council in 2006.

The aims of the project were to grow a large number of Irish fleabane plants at the National Botanic Gardens and then plant these into selected sites along the shores of Lough Derg. Over the following months and years the local community along with the other project partners monitored and tended the newly planted populations. In years to come it was hoped that these new populations of Irish fleabane would insure that an important part of Ireland’s biodiversity is not lost from the shores of the lake forever. 

On the 15th May 2006, Jim Martin delivered plants to the Botanic Gardens from Terryglass. These were divided and grown on by Joan Rogers in ca. 42 five-inch pots in Tree & Shrub mix. Some of these re replanted on the shores of Lough Derg but unfortunately many of the plants were destroyed when the Shannon flooded over the past number of years.

The National Botanic gardens kindly gave us some plants which we hoped to plant this year during the rally however they are not strong enough yet and they are currently in the care of our green fingered vice-commodore and when they are stronger we hope to have them planted on the shores of Lough Derg (Maybe during Rally 38).
