**The Commodores Boat**

Prepared for Rally Magazine 28 (2003)

Vickers in Dublin built 68M in 1936 and like all other workboats on the Canal before she could go into service she had to be weighed and calibrated. Denis Crowe did this on July the 8th on the boat slip in Killaloe, the tonnage marks can still be seen on the bow and stern posts. When weighed the following stores were aboard, 40 planks, 6 covers, 2 poles, 1 boat hook, 3 rope fenders, 1 cork fender, 2 stop ropes, 2 hand ropes, 3 lash lines, 2 trippers, 1 empty cask, 1 anchor and 120 gallons of fuel oil for the Bollinder engine.

It then went into service with the Grand Canal Company (GCC) carrying general cargo including porter for Guinness’s. Paddy "Waxer" Dunne (Lowtown) was skipper of 68M for a while says that she worked mostly on the Dublin - Limerick route.

Some of the people that worked on 68M were Dick Kearney as a greaser in 1939, at that time the crew were Paddy Connolly (Killina), Tom Hilbert (Ballyteague) and Gannon Melia (Ticknevin). Later on Dick moved to 35M and the next time he was back on 68M Amby Dwyer (Newcastle), Tom Doonican (Pollagh) and Banagher Tom (Tom Carroll) were the crew. Banagher Jack (Tom's brother) also worked on 68M for a while, as did Jack Kearney (Dicks brother).

Matt Smullen says that Gannon Melia was 68M's skipper from circ 1943-44 to 1946-47. Paddy Kearney (Dicks brother) and Bill Cox worked on her after that circ 1947-48, also around that time Joe Connolly (Shannon Harbour) was her skipper for a couple of months while his boat was in dry dock. Tommy Anderson was her last skipper from circ 1954 to 1958 when she was eventually retired from commercial operations. 68M was de-commissioned with the rest of the fleet in 1960, however while most of the boats were later sold off by CIE at auction, 68M was kept and some years later was back working on the canal.

Her second lease of life was as a canal maintenance gravel boat when she operated as such from the early 1960's to the late 1970's. Technically when she worked as a gravel boat on maintenance of the canal she should have been re numbered to an "E" (Engineering) boat, we presume this didn't happen as all commercial activity had ceased on the canals by then. Jimmy Conroy the lock keeper in Lowtown says that Willy Anger was her skipper in the 1970's. During this time the original fifteen-horse power Bollinder engine was replaced by a sixty-horse power Lister air-cooled diesel engine. The Bollinder subsequently ended up in the England. Sometime around 1980, 68M had her engine removed and was left to rust away with a number of other barges partially submerged in the Grand Canal at Ballycommon. Because of vandalism they were all taken up some years later and transferred to Tullamore harbour for safe keeping were she sank and remained under water until August 93.
