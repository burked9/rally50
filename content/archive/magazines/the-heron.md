At rest, standing on one leg with eyes half closed and head hunched between its shoulders, the heron seems hardly to be interested in the world around it. But it takes only the rippling of an eel in the shallows or the rustling of a mouse moving through the grass to summon the lightning reflexes which have made the bird one of the most efficient killers of the marshes.

The heron's hunting technique is to stalk through the shallows, ready to strike at prey with its pick-axe of a bill. So successful is this method that anglers used to rub fat from the bird onto their bait, in the belief that the heron's feet and legs put out a special oil which had the power of attracting fish. In fact, the bird has no need of mysterious oils, for it has something just as effective...endless patience.

The heron will eat fish, eels, frogs, mice or water-voles. Small fish are swallowed whole, head first, and larger ones are stabbed repeatedly then taken to the bank to have the flesh picked from their bones.

Herons will range more than 12 miles for food. Sometimes they raid garden ponds for goldfish, at others they may visit farmyards and cornfields, searching for rodents. They inhabit large areas of the River Shannon, particularly around Lough Derg. Once a heronry is established, birds will return to the same site year after year. They have a wide and raucous vocabulary, with the most common call a loud, harsh 'knornk'. Courtship is preceded by a dance ceremony in which the male stretches its neck up and then lowers it over its back, with bill pointing upwards. When there are eggs or nestlings to care for, one parent stands on guard against predators while the other is away feeding.

Only one species of heron breeds in Ireland, and it is called the Grey Heron, to distinguish it from the Purple Heron, which breeds in Holland and sometimes visits Ireland on migration. It is estimated that there are about 4000 breeding pairs between Ireland and the UK. Most herons stay present in Ireland throughout the year, but some have been known to migrate to coasts during the winter.   

### Some Facts about the Grey Heron:
- Large, long necked bird with a long, stout, pointed bill.
- Adults show a white head and neck with a black crown stripe which forms a loose crest. Black stripes obvious on the foreneck extending onto loose shaggy breast.
- Upperparts blue-grey with a shoulder patch and pale elongated scapulars forming plumes. Black on sides of breast continues down as flank stripe, meeting on the ventral area.
- Flanks and belly white. Bill orange-yellow.
- Flies on bowed wings, showing contrasting grey wing coverts and blackish primaries and secondaries.
- Flies with neck retracted and trailing, dull-greenish legs.
