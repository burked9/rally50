**Latin name: *Alcedo Atthis***

The kingfisher is one of the many birds that can be found along the River Shannon. Their bodies are short and plump, and their legs and feet are small. It grows to be 16-17 cm of which the bill takes up about 4cm and it weighs 35-40grms. It is bright blue and green upper plumage, rusty orange beneath, pattern of red, white and blue on cheeks; bright red feet; long beak is black in males, has red underside in females.

### Habitat and Nesting
Its habitat is clean, slow moving or still water with suitable banks for nesting. They nest in tunnels a meter long dug into earth or sandy riverbanks. They lay six or seven roundish pure white eggs, these are laid between April & July and incubation is time is about twenty days. It is not unusual for a pair to have two or even three broods in a year. The nest is in a depression at the end of the tunnel and is lined with only old fish bones. The young begin to fledge after ten days and can fly twenty-five days after hatching. The young will quickly make the hole messy and smelly, so once they fledge the nest will be abandoned and a new one drilled next year.

### Diet and Identification
It eats small fish, minnows, fry and occasionally insects. It is one of the best fishing birds with a dagger beak and hunts from perches above water. Its habits are, to wait patiently on its perch and dives expertly into the water.

The red base of the lower mandible of her beak can identify the female Kingfisher. The male’s bill is all black. Adult Kingfishers have bright red feet; on young birds they are much darker.

### Conservation
Once persecuted by fishermen, Kingfishers declined through much of their range. Now the species enjoys full legal protection and must not be harmed in any way.
