Summary: A tribute to Eric Timon, a legendary figure in the Inland Waterways Association of Ireland.

![Eric Timon]({static}/images/people/memorials/eric_timon.png)

*From Rally Magazine 37 (2012)*

As a teenager in a barber shop at Sutton Cross, I was told the story of a boat builder who had named his creation AMOW (all my own work). I am sure because I was bending his ear about fishing off the Baily lighthouse in my father’s boat. It was some years before I was welcomed into a circle of ESB people that included Eric, who were involved in IWAI and all that went with it.

Eric to me was the elder lemon with stories of free fuel on Shannon Rallies and of Fitz-er and Syd on the barges and of course rally after rally. As a group, our involvement in canals at every level was huge but in the seventies the “elder elder lemons” were still around to spur on the work parties and boat trips for the public to further the cause. Eric and myself had Howth in common and shared our inland and offshore interests, a big difference however was his prowess at water ski-ing which he excelled at. His imagination and writing abilities are legendary and Pookas and dittys became standard rally speak.

A real little powerhouse in reality, as Eric moved from AMOW to NAMOWS (many!) and produced books that are an historical record of boating over decades. Everyone had an Eric story and they were better if told by the man himself. Our crew penned a ditty once about nervous sailors (the calm calm club) which had a line about the eighty something Timon in the thick of it, but then there were men, and real men. Some years back, the honour bestowed on himself and Chris Boyle as the only people who entered all of the Derg rallies to that date, was gratefully and gracefully received.

Personally I have lost a true friend of the “four in the morning giving help” type which is a rare enough thing and I know I am not alone. His family of course are at a bigger loss but our times with Eric will make up for this. I would like to finish with his own words from the foreword of the Namow book which he published in 1996.

> “This book was written with all my friends in mind, especially those in whose company I cruised the canals and the river Shannon over the past thirty years. Should it by any unfortunate mischance fall into the hands of a ‘land-lubber’ who dares to read it, the only hope he has of avoiding the Pooka’s curse, is to purchase, rent, hire, beg, borrow or steal a boat and join the local branch of the IWAI immediately”.

Great times and memories, thank you so much Eric.
— Joe

*Eric Timon passed away in June this year sadly missed by his loving wife, family, relatives and friends. R.I.P.*
