This recording was made by **Martin Burke**, who was the piper aboard JJ Kenneally's boat *Carmel*, and was kindly shared by his son, Ronan. Martin Burke was the first to start the beloved Rally tradition of waking everyone up in the morning with the pipes.

Martin used to play the Irish War Pipes, which Ronan had restored recently, having originally been made in 1890.

---

### Lyrics

Oh we sailed up to Garry, from there to Tuamgraney, then nudged up to Scarriff's stone quay  
Back the river next morning, hit a storm without warning, then sheltered near Red in the lee  
Our next port of call was Mountshannon, two points southeast of Clore hill  
Oh we tied up our craft, both fore and aft, and went for a jar up the hill  

Oh we're cruising and sailing Lough Derg every year  
We call to each port and drink plenty of beer  
In small ships and tall ships and barges and all  
At Lough Derg's boat rally, we're having a ball  

Between Cribby and Bushy, on starboard is Lushing, our bows face for Youghal broad bay  
Past Scillys and Parkers, past black and red markers, we cast off at dawn every day  
The Hare you can see through the mist and the fog, Ryan's Point is not very clear  
Carrigeen island so bright and serene, we're gliding into Dromineer  

Oh the Whiskey's Still aft and we're heading up north, away from the southern shore  
Past Benjy and Tinkers, in carvel and clinkers, Kilgarvan our landfall by four  
South of the Split, the wind it did blow, a westerly wave made us roll  
We steadied her bow and shook herself free, and carried straight in to the foam  

Just up from Kilgarvan the crew told the captain, I think we're aground near Gortmore  
The beer is all gone and the grub's running low, it's best we abandon our boat  
Stay at your post the skipper replied, I'll get you out of this mess  
Give me an oar, a boat-hook or gaff, and I'll scull you into Terryglass  

From Portumna north down to Killaloe south, we sail up and down this great lake  
From Clanricarde's shore down to Kincora's shore, behind us a beautiful wake  
If when the day comes that I'm an oul man, my golden anchor I'll cast  
Out in the deep, far out from the shore, where my youthful days were all passed  

---

### Notes & Landmarks of the Lake
* **Red:** Red Island (Scarriff Bay)
* **Lushing:** Lushing Rocks
* **Hare:** Hare Island
* **Split:** Split Rock
* **Benjy (Benjamin):** Benjamin Rock (named after the first person to sound Lough Derg)
* **The Whiskey Still:** A pub in Dromineer
