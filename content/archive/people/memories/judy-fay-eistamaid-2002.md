**EISTAMAID MORNING**

Judy Fay

Sunday morning, Portumna Castle Harbour, and the bloody birds woke me again. I was immediately aware of the stuffiness of the cabin and the taste of too much of last night's wine in my mouth. My sister Cathy had stayed in the Hotel down the road the previous night, and I had no faith that she would remember to arrive on time. Trying not to wake the snoring corpses scattered all around me, I retrieved the plastic bag of ducks and masks from its hiding place, and hunted for the sellotape and scissors. The little party masks looked insubstantial and a bit silly, and I wondered whether the idea, which had seemed so funny when conceived in a giggly moment, would end up as a soggy joke.

The Lough Derg Rally is an annual event on the River Shannon, where a collection of boats of all sizes and shapes travel together around the lake from place to place. In theory the purpose of the rally is to foster safety on the water and to educate people in boat handling. In practice people go because it is great craic. There are competitions, singsongs, music and drink. Everything is arranged by a Committee that works hard during the year to make the event a success. On the last night there is a gala dinner (hence too much wine) and the following morning is devoted to the Eistamaid competition. This competition is open only to members of the Committee. I have no idea why it is called the Eistamaid. The winner is given a silver trophy for a year, and their name is inscribed on it for posterity. Neither Cathy nor I is on the Committee, but we have attended the rally for years, and are occasionally asked to lend a hand. This year we were asked to arrange the Eistamaid competition (bad idea!).

We started with two objectives, that the competition would be a good spectator sport for those not involved, and that all those taking part would have a better than even chance of getting wet. Our first thought was to toss coloured ping-pong balls into the lake and get committee members to canoe out and pick them up. We hit a slight practical difficulty early in the planning. Obviously they don't play table tennis in Portumna. But the £1 shop turned up trumps. A family of yellow plastic ducks, two large and four small, fitted the bill perfectly. We thought that we would put two people into each canoe, one could paddle and one collect ducks. But these were experienced boaters, and simple canoeing would present no serious challenge. How then to handicap them and increase the chance of their getting wet. Cathy had the bright idea of having one person paddling in the back of the canoe, blindfold, and the other giving directions from the front, who would also collect the ducks. The canoes in question are long American Indian open canoes, and can be easy to overturn. For safety reasons the blindfolds had to be easy to remove if the person fell in, so we chose little cardboard party masks, covering the eyeholes with paper and tape.

Apart from the effects of the previous night's party, it was a dull overcast morning, and people were slow to leave their bunks. The grass beside the marina was damp and slippery as we struggled to lift the canoe from the roof of Chang-sha. The other canoe had to be borrowed, Joe Treacy was getting dressed when we knocked on the door of 4E, and he came out, grinning, fastening the belt on his trousers.

"What are you going to do to us today? No more eggs I hope?" he joked.
The last time we ran this event most of the participants ended up with egg in their hair. Cathy told Joe to fetch his life jacket and call the rest of the committee members on his way. Chris Boyle was standing on the quayside cracking jokes about my incompetence with ropes and gang planks.
"Your time will come," Cathy retorted.

Angie Dwane and Christine Becker set off in the rubber dinghy to disperse the ducks. The wash from the outboard made little waves that gently rocked the canoes.

Martina Egan was first into the canoe with Joe Treacy, protesting that she didn't need to be blindfolded as she wasn't wearing her glasses, and therefore couldn't see.

Tom McGowan clambered down the rocks. Stepping awkwardly into the middle of the canoe, it rocked and he fell into the water laughing. Climbing out, his hair and clothes dripping, he tried again. This time his wet shoes slipped on the rocks and again he ended up in the water. Third time he stepped in gingerly, holding on tightly when the canoe tilted, and he finally made it to a sitting position. His partner Matt Gallagher, pristine as always, looked on in horror.
"Are you planning on swimming for them?" he asked.
The thought of getting wet obviously didn't appeal.

Kneeling at the front of the canoe Matt called instructions to Tom who was blindfolded and paddling.
"Left a bit... No, I said left... Right a bit... Straight ahead... Back."
It was like the Golden Shot. A whole lot of paddling and several detours later, they had just one duck perched on the canoe. Meanwhile, Martina and Joe had it down to a fine art. Their communication and co-ordination meant that they had cleaned up all the other ducks while Tom and Matt were still finding their way out of the reeds.

Markie Becker and Joe Leonard won their heat easily and met Martina and Joe in the finals. To make things a little more difficult, Angie revved up the outboard and made some waves. At first the teams appeared to be well matched, until Markie leaned a little too far to the left and fell in, the canoe toppled, and Joe Leonard was soon in the water with him. They tried to right it, but it was full of water and very heavy. So they decided instead to sabotage the opposition. They swam for the ducks, managing to retrieve both of the large ones and two small ones. But they came unstuck when they tried to retrieve the canoe and bring it back to shore. Have you ever tried to swim in a wet lifejacket, pulling a canoe, while holding an armful of plastic ducks?
Martina came alongside leaning over to reclaim the booty. Markie grabbed her lifejacket, and all four of them were in the water, spluttering and laughing. A fight for the ducks ensued, in which it was impossible to see who had the upper hand.

Finally, Markie and Joe, ever the tacticians, counted their spoils and, finding a majority, headed for home. A well-deserved victory it was too, with additional marks awarded all round for artistic impression.
